Our position

Adventurous in design.
Disciplined on site.

Innovation is not permission to experiment with a client's programme or safety. We are open to emerging products and new design languages, but selection should be based on evidence, suitability and a dependable installation method.

Our selection framework

Six questions before a new product gets specified.

01

What problem does it solve?

Start with the operational, spatial or aesthetic benefit — not the novelty.

02

Does it suit this building?

Check interfaces, dimensions, loads, environmental conditions and compatibility with existing systems.

03

What evidence supports it?

Request relevant product data, test information, certifications and manufacturer guidance appropriate to the proposed use.

04

Who knows how to install it?

Confirm competent installers, approved methods, sequencing and manufacturer requirements.

05

Can it be maintained?

Consider access, replacement parts, warranties, cleaning and how it will age in a working office.

06

What is the fallback?

Keep viable alternatives where procurement, approval or performance is uncertain.

Where we stay curious

Useful innovation, not novelty for its own sake.

01 / MATERIALS

New surfaces and finishes

Lower-impact options, robust decorative finishes, modular systems and materials that can be repaired or replaced.

02 / LIGHT

Lighting that adapts

Efficient fittings, controls, scene-setting and user comfort, coordinated with the electrical design and installation requirements.

03 / PARTITIONS

Spaces that can change

Glazed partitions, acoustic solutions and demountable elements considered alongside fire, acoustic and building constraints.

04 / TECHNOLOGY

Better-connected workplaces

Practical AV, meeting-room controls and technology that works reliably for the people using it.

The BAMBU standard

Bold enough to explore. Experienced enough to question.

We will never describe a product as suitable, compliant or proven for a particular project without the relevant checks. Design ambition is strongest when it survives technical scrutiny.
