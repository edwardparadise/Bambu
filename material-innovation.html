Materials & specification

Look closer at the finish.

A tabletop can be refinished. A floor tile can be lifted. Decisions about wear, repair and replacement start with the first sample.

From sample to specification

  1. 01

    Keep what works

    Inspect the floor, partitions and furniture. Record what can stay, what needs repair and what can move.

  2. 02

    Test the sample

    View it under the proposed lighting. Check wear, cleaning, compatibility and installation against the supplier’s data.

  3. 03

    Plan for replacement

    Check lead times, spare stock and access. Agree how the maintenance team will repair a damaged panel or tile.

Project assessment

Which standard fits the brief?

Agree the target with the landlord and project team before design starts. These independent schemes assess individual projects. Any rating depends on formal assessment.

SKArating
Benchmarks a fit-out against good practice. An accredited assessor identifies the measures that apply to the work and verifies the evidence for certification.
BREEAM
Assesses the environmental performance of refurbishment and fit-out work. A licensed assessor can confirm the route for a tenant floor, landlord works or a wider refurbishment.
WELL
Focuses on how the building supports health and wellbeing. Performance verification includes on-site testing of conditions such as air quality, light and sound.
Fitwel
Uses a scorecard of design and operational measures that support health. The appropriate scorecard depends on the building and the space covered by the project.

Product thinking: Explore. Validate. Install. Test suitability, data and maintenance before specifying.

Product in use

A light belongs to a desk.

Check the reach of the arm, glare on adjacent screens and where the cable will run. A fitting that looks right in isolation still has to work for the person sitting beside it.
