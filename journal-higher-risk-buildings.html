Higher-risk buildings (HRBs): a guide to office fit-out approvals

Planning an office refurbishment or commercial fit-out in London? Establish whether the building falls within the higher-risk building (HRB) regime before signing a lease or agreeing a construction programme. Under the Building Safety Act framework, this can affect building control approval, fire safety design and project timescales.

What is a Higher-Risk Building?

In England, during building work, the regime generally covers buildings at least 18 metres high or with seven or more storeys that contain two or more residential units, or are or contain a hospital or care home. The statutory rules for measuring height, counting storeys and defining the building matter.

A purely commercial office tower is not automatically an HRB. An office within a qualifying mixed-use development may be affected. The separate in-occupation regime has a different definition.

How does Building Safety Regulator (BSR) approval work?

  1. Establish. Confirm the building’s legal status, including its use, height and configuration. An office-only tower does not qualify simply because it is tall.
  2. Assess. Define the works with the landlord and competent designers. Assess escape routes, compartmentation, sprinklers and fire alarms. Establish whether approval is required.
  3. Approve. Classify regulated work as category A or B as applicable. Coordinate the drawings and compliance information. Obtain Building Safety Regulator approval before starting work that requires it.
  4. Construct. Maintain compliance records and manage changes. Notify the regulator where required. Obtain approval before proceeding with work affected by a major change.
  5. Complete. Collect test results, commissioning records and as-built information. Apply for a completion certificate where required and hand over relevant building safety information.

Not all work requires an application. Certain exempt works, competent person scheme works and qualifying fibre optic cabling have exceptions. If they form part of a wider regulated project, they may need to be included in that application. Landlord consent is separate from regulatory approval.

Does an office fit-out need HRB approval?

Not simply because the premises are in a tall building. Establish whether the building meets the statutory definition and whether the proposed work is subject to building control approval. Review the scope with the landlord and competent dutyholders. A landlord’s consent does not replace BSR approval.

How long does HRB approval take for an office refurbishment?

The statutory decision period for an existing HRB building control application is eight weeks, unless a longer period is agreed. This is not a guaranteed approval date and excludes design preparation. Allow for regulatory queries and the completion certificate process as well.

Planning your office fit-out: ask the difficult questions early.

Identify the building’s status and approval route before fixing the programme or committing to construction. Review the landlord’s requirements and licence to alter, then allow for technical design and regulatory approval in your fit-out programme.
